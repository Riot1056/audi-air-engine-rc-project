What counts as real progress?

01 • DefineConfirm printer, available materials, school rules, budget, and how each target will be measured.
02 • Make it moveRedesign the CAD into separate parts; hand-turn the crank and check alignment, friction, and fit.
03 • Measure, then decideOnly plan a pneumatic test with a teacher-reviewed setup and commercially rated pressure hardware. Record results before moving toward the car.
Safety and honesty: This printed concept is not a pressure-rated engine, cylinder, or tank. Do not connect it to compressed air. The decorative piston grooves are not a specified seal. Any later pneumatic testing needs commercially rated hardware and qualified adult/teacher review.

Questions that will make the project stronger

What is the first pass/fail test?
For example: can a separately printed piston-crank assembly turn freely by hand through a full rotation?
Which requirement is non-negotiable?
Mass, speed, or runtime may need to change once air use and component mass are measured.
What will the test record?
Version, print settings, fit, mass, crank speed, failures, and the next design change.
Who can review the setup?
Ask a teacher or pneumatics mentor before selecting any pressurized components.
